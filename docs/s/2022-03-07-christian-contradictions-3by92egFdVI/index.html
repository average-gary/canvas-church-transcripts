Big idea

How we treat others—especially those who bring nothing to the table—reveals whether we truly live by the gospel of grace or are merely moral religious people building earthly kingdoms through discrimination.

Summary

This sermon on James 1:26–2:13 argues that discrimination—treating people differently based on external factors like wealth, race, or status—exposes a fundamental contradiction in the Christian life. The preacher contends that showing favoritism reveals we are valuing earthly rewards over kingdom values, living for the wrong kingdom, and hypocritically judging others by the law while claiming grace for ourselves.

Key points

  • Discrimination reveals we value what people can do for us rather than their intrinsic worth
  • The kingdom of God is built on mercy and faith, not external status or power
  • We judge ourselves by grace but judge others by law—a hypocritical contradiction
  • Biblical love means inconveniencing yourself for others, especially those who cannot repay
  • Mercy triumphs over judgment; we are called to extend the grace we have received
