Big idea

Revelation was written to help first-century Christians see past Roman imperial propaganda and remain faithful witnesses to Christ despite cultural and economic pressure, and reading it in that original context unlocks how it challenges us to resist being co-opted by any nation's temporal agenda.

Summary

Dr. David DeSilva teaches that Revelation should be read primarily as a prophetic letter to seven first-century churches in Roman Asia, challenging them to faithful witness amid imperial propaganda and economic pressure, rather than as a predictive roadmap for distant future events. He argues that understanding Revelation in its original context of Roman emperor worship, polytheism, and trade networks unlocks its challenge to Christians living under any empire's competing narrative about power and prosperity.

Key points

  • Revelation is a letter to real churches facing specific first-century pressures, not primarily about distant future predictions
  • Prophecy is God's word diagnosing present circumstances and calling for realignment, not mainly prediction
  • The seven churches lived surrounded by emperor worship, traditional gods' temples, and economic incentives to compromise
  • John unveils how God sees Roman imperial power versus Rome's propaganda about peace and prosperity
  • Reading with the original audience helps us resist seduction by temporal political and economic agendas today
