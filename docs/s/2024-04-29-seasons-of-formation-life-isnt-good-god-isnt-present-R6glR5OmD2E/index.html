SEASONS OF FORMATION: Life Isn't Good, God Isn't Present

Date2024-04-29
Length00:56:53
Words7,109
SeriesSEASON OF FORMATION
VideoYouTube ↗

Big idea

God uses seasons of darkness and his seeming absence not as punishment but as a gift to purge hidden sin and form us into Christ's likeness, requiring us to choose faith in what we learned in the light even as we doubt in the darkness.

Summary

This sermon explores the 'dark night of the soul'—seasons when life is hard and God seems absent—as part of God's formative work rather than punishment. Drawing on Benjamin Hastings' crisis of faith during Hillsong's collapse, the pastor argues that God uses these painful seasons to purge hidden pride and sin, transforming believers through a process of spiritual refining that feels like crushing grapes to make new wine.

Key points

  • Benjamin Hastings' journey from worship-leader success to doubt during Hillsong's leadership scandals
  • Biblical examples of the dark night: David, Asaph, Mother Teresa questioning God's presence
  • God purges the 'unknown' quadrant—hidden sins only He sees—through painful seasons
  • Two spiritual disciplines for darkness: praise (declaring truth over feelings) and lament (honest, uncensored prayer to God)
  • The prayer 'break me, melt me, mold me, fill me' invites transformation we may not be ready for
