A SUMMER'S JOURNEY: The Sin of Religious Certainty

Date2024-05-13
Length01:04:13

Big idea

Our intellectual certainty and self-righteousness can blind us to who God truly is, causing us to fight against Him even while claiming to serve Him.

Summary

This sermon introduces a summer series on Paul's life by examining his early role as Saul the persecutor at Stephen's martyrdom in Acts 7-8. The message explores how Saul's religious certainty, intellectual pride, and self-righteousness as a Pharisee caused him to violently oppose the very Messiah he claimed to be waiting for, even as Stephen demonstrated Christ-like love by laying down his life.

Key points

  • Saul's identity as a Pharisee shaped his zealous persecution of the early church
  • Five Jewish responses to Roman occupation reveal different approaches to faithfulness
  • Stephen's martyrdom mirrored Jesus' crucifixion, yet Saul remained blind to the truth
  • Religious certainty and self-righteousness can cause us to miss God's actual work
  • We must respond to disagreement like Stephen (sacrificial love) not Saul (angry stubbornness)
