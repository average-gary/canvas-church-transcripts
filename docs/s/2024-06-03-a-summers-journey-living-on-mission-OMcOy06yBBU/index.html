Big idea

True discipleship requires relational investment in mentors, peers, and disciples, and faithful obedience in small things can have profound kingdom impact even without prominence.

Summary

This sermon examines Barnabas as a model disciple-maker who faithfully invested in Paul through relational mentoring, not just information transfer. The speaker challenges the church to practice authentic discipleship by surrounding themselves with mentors, peers, and those they disciple, emphasizing that prominence does not equal importance in God's kingdom.

Key points

  • Barnabas was not Paul's sidekick but a mature disciple who mentored and recruited Paul
  • Barnabas demonstrated obedience through selling his property, interceding for Paul, and stepping out of the limelight
  • Christians should surround themselves with three types: mentors above, peers beside, and disciples below
  • Discipleship is relational and holistic, not merely transferring information
  • The cost of non-discipleship is greater than the cost of following Jesus
