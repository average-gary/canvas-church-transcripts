Big idea

Genuine saving faith in Christ will inevitably produce good works as evidence - not to earn salvation, but to complete and demonstrate the faith that saves us.

Summary

This sermon examines James 2:14-26 on the relationship between faith and works, arguing that genuine saving faith will inevitably produce good works as evidence of its authenticity. Andy distinguishes between dead faith (merely intellectual assent or empty words) and living faith (trust in God that results in obedience and action), using Abraham and Rahab as biblical examples.

Key points

  • Dead faith takes three forms: claiming faith without action, offering thoughts and prayers without help, and believing truth intellectually without transformation
  • Living faith is demonstrated through Abraham (who reasoned about God's character and obeyed) and Rahab (who believed and acted by hiding the spies)
  • We are saved by faith alone, but not by faith that is alone - works demonstrate genuine faith without earning salvation
  • Producing good works requires remaining in Christ like branches on a vine, practicing through repeated small decisions, and giving fruit time to grow
  • Self-examination is necessary to assess whether our faith is alive by looking for the fruit of the Spirit in our character and actions
