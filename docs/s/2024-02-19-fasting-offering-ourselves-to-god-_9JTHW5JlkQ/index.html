Big idea

Fasting is a practice aimed at offering our entire selves—body and spirit—to God so that He can transform us into the image of Jesus.

Summary

This sermon introduces fasting as a spiritual discipline for offering our whole selves—body and spirit—to God. The pastor addresses Western resistance to fasting, provides historical context from Moses through the early church, answers practical questions about what fasting is and how to practice it, and grounds the practice theologically in Romans 12:1-2 as a way to present our bodies as living sacrifices for transformation into Christ's likeness.

Key points

  • Fasting has deep roots in Jewish and Christian tradition, though largely neglected in the modern Western church
  • Biblical fasting means abstaining from food for a set time, not giving up other comforts or restricted diets
  • Western Christians are influenced by hedonism (overvaluing physical comfort) and Gnosticism (undervaluing the body), both distortions of the gospel
  • Fasting is 'body talk'—a physical practice with spiritual implications that unites body and spirit in worship
  • This is an invitation, not a command; those with health or eating disorder concerns should practice abstinence from other things instead
