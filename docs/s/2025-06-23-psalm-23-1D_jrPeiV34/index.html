Big idea

God is our faithful shepherd who provides everything we need, protects us through the darkest valleys, and relentlessly pursues us with his goodness and mercy all the days of our lives.

Summary

This sermon explores Psalm 23 through the lens of ancient Near Eastern shepherding practices, explaining how the metaphor of God as shepherd would have resonated with the original audience. The preacher uses archaeological research, field studies with Bedouin shepherds, and detailed knowledge of sheep behavior to illuminate each verse, ultimately pointing to Jesus as the Good Shepherd who lays down his life for the sheep.

Key points

  • The Book of Psalms is a curated collection spanning 1000+ years with multiple authors, designed to help God's people worship
  • Ancient shepherding was dangerous, isolated work requiring constant vigilance, knowledge of terrain and water sources, and willingness to defend the flock
  • Sheep are nearsighted, defenseless, prone to panic when lost, and completely dependent on their shepherd for survival
  • Psalm 23 moves from shepherd imagery to gracious host, with God's goodness and mercy actively pursuing (chasing down) believers
  • Jesus fulfills the role of Good Shepherd that failed human leaders could not, seeking the scattered sheep and laying down his life for them
