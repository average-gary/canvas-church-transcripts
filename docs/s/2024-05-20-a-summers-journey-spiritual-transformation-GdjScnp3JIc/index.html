Big idea

God is in the business of redirecting people from their way to the way, and He often uses us as guides along the way.

Summary

This sermon examines Saul's conversion on the road to Damascus (Acts 9), using it to illustrate how God lovingly redirects people from their own destructive path ('my way') to His life-giving path ('the way'). The message emphasizes that Christian identity should be marked by the direction of one's life, not merely intellectual belief, and culminates in a call to baptism as the symbol of dying to self and being reborn in Christ.

Key points

  • Early Christians were called 'followers of the way' - identity defined by life direction, not just belief
  • Only two spiritual directions exist: God's way (narrow road to life) or our own way (wide road to destruction)
  • God lovingly confronts us when we're headed the wrong direction, as He did with Saul on the Damascus road
  • God uses ordinary believers like Ananias to participate in redirecting others and extending grace
  • No one is too far gone for God's grace - Saul the persecutor became Paul the apostle
