Big idea

Communion is an intentional, embodied rhythm that physically connects believers with Jesus and one another, inviting them into the mystery of koinonia with Christ's body and blood to combat spiritual distraction and relational disconnection.

Summary

This sermon examines communion from a fresh perspective, exploring its biblical titles, purpose, and practice. The pastor argues that communion was given to combat spiritual distraction and relational disconnection, and that believers should approach it reflectively, regularly, and expectantly, embracing the mystery of participating (koinonia) in Christ's body and blood.

Key points

  • Five biblical titles for communion reveal its depth: Lord's Supper, Breaking of Bread, Eucharist, Love Feast, and Communion
  • Jesus gave communion to keep believers focused on the cross and connected to God and each other
  • Communion involves mysterious koinonia (intimate fellowship) with Christ's body and blood beyond mere symbolism
  • Believers should approach the table reflectively (examining sin and broken relationships), regularly and corporately (not just individually), and expectantly (anticipating God's presence)
  • Communion was meant to unite the church but has tragically become a source of division throughout history
