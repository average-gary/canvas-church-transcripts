RUTH: Redemption Through Suffering

Big idea

God brings redemption in the midst of chaos and suffering to anyone who clings to Him in faith, though this redemption is often slow and messy and doesn't erase the pain of living in a broken world.

Summary

This sermon introduces the book of Ruth as more than a simple love story—it's a microcosm of the entire biblical narrative, set during the chaotic time of Judges. The pastor argues that Ruth demonstrates how God brings redemption through suffering to those who cling to Him in faith, while acknowledging the complexity and slowness of that redemption in a broken world.

Key points

  • Set in Judges era when 'everyone did what was right in their own eyes,' leading to famine and chaos
  • Elimelech's family experiences total loss—death of husband and sons leaves Naomi and Ruth as widows
  • Rebellion against God produces three consequences: struggle for food, struggle for family, and death
  • God's redemption is real but painstakingly slow, messy, and doesn't erase suffering on this side of heaven
  • Ruth, a Gentile woman, shows redemption is for anyone who clings to God in faith; the story ends with Obed in David's lineage leading to Jesus
