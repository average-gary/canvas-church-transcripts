Big idea

Unless the Lord builds the house, all human effort is empty; true peace and success come only from resting in God's love and aiming our lives—and our children's lives—at faithfulness to Christ rather than worldly achievement.

Summary

This sermon examines Psalm 127 through Solomon's life, contrasting worldly success achieved through human effort with godly success rooted in God's strength. Michelle Meeks teaches that all striving apart from God is 'vanity' (emptiness), and challenges the church to shape children as 'arrows' aimed at God's definition of success rather than the world's.

Key points

  • Solomon started humbly seeking wisdom but ended worshiping idols when he relied on his own strength
  • After Eden's fall, humanity lost peace and gained anxiety, fear, and endless striving
  • True rest comes from trusting God's love, not from accomplishments or toil
  • The Lamb (Jesus) reigns through sacrificial love, revealing the path to real victory
  • Children are arrows to aim at godly success: faithfulness, relationship with Christ, and obedience to His word
