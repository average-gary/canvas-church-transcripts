Big idea

The church's identity as saved people includes both being redeemed to God and being reconciled to one another across ethnic and cultural lines, and both dimensions are essential—not secondary—to embodying the kingdom of heaven on earth.

Summary

This sermon explores Ephesians 2 to argue that salvation encompasses both a vertical dimension (redemption to God) and a horizontal dimension (reconciliation across ethnic and cultural boundaries). Using a tricycle metaphor, the pastor contends that the church has overemphasized individual salvation while neglecting God's call to multi-ethnic unity, resulting in Sunday morning being the most segregated time in America.

Key points

  • Salvation includes both vertical redemption to God and horizontal reconciliation to one another
  • Jesus fulfilled Israel's covenant purpose to be a blessing to all nations
  • Believers are raised above earthly powers and seated with Christ in heavenly places
  • The church should embody multi-ethnic, multicultural unity as a picture of heaven
  • American churches have failed to live out the horizontal reconciliation aspect of salvation
