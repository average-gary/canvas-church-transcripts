Big idea

The church must embrace creative, Spirit-led innovation in methods to reach people far from God, while maintaining the unchanging gospel message and prioritizing humility and unity over results.

Summary

This sermon explores the 'pilgrim' spiritual station—believers who are creative, action-oriented, and willing to adapt their methods (while preserving the gospel message) to reach people far from God. Using Acts 15 as the primary text, the preacher examines the early church's debate over Gentile conversion requirements and argues that the church must embrace innovation and give pilgrims a stronger voice while maintaining humility and unity.

Key points

  • Pilgrims do whatever works to reach people far from God, adapting methods while keeping the message constant
  • Acts 15 shows pilgrims look for the Holy Spirit's movement, listen to testimonies, and remain accountable to Scripture
  • Shadow sides include getting stuck in past innovations, losing sight of the message, and sacrificing unity for results
  • Healthy pilgrims innovate with humility for the sake of church unity
  • Jesus is the ultimate pilgrim who fulfilled the old covenant while creating something new
