Big idea

In moments when we feel most forsaken by God, we must remember that God has not abandoned us—just as He restored David and raised Jesus, He will bring us through if we trust the full story to its end.

Summary

This sermon explores Psalm 22, written by David during a time of feeling abandoned, and its prophetic fulfillment in Jesus' crucifixion when He cried out 'My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?' The preacher demonstrates how the gospel writers saw detailed parallels between the psalm and Jesus' suffering, and encourages believers to pray through the entire psalm—from lament to triumph—when they feel forsaken.

Key points

  • David wrote Psalm 22 during intense suffering, possibly during Absalom's coup, feeling God had abandoned him despite his faithfulness
  • Jesus quoted the psalm's opening line from the cross, invoking the entire song in listeners' minds
  • The gospel writers recorded numerous parallels: mocking crowds, divided garments, pierced hands and feet, lions and dogs imagery
  • The psalm transitions from despair to prophetic triumph—proclaiming an eternal kingdom and resurrection
  • When believers feel abandoned, they must pray the psalm to its end, trusting God's faithfulness as shown in David's restoration and Christ's resurrection
