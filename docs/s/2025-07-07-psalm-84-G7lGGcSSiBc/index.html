Big idea

Genuine praise of God requires not just longing for His presence but actively journeying towards it through suffering, sustained by the vision of eternal restoration rather than temporary comfort.

Summary

This sermon examines Psalm 84 as a three-movement journey: longing for God's presence, journeying towards it through suffering, and ultimately proclaiming genuine praise. The preacher explores why the psalm's famous declaration 'better is one day in your courts than thousands elsewhere' can feel aspirational or even hypocritical when we are not actively pursuing God's presence in our daily lives.

Key points

  • The Sons of Korah, redeemed from a heritage of rebellion, wrote this pilgrimage psalm
  • Longing for God's presence must move us to action, not remain intellectual
  • The journey towards God passes through the 'valley of Baca' (weeping and suffering)
  • We move from strength to strength by focusing on eternal redemption, not temporary comfort
  • Genuine praise requires actually journeying towards God, not just thinking about it
