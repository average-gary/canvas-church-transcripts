Righteous Anger?

Date2022-02-21
Length00:37:29
Words4,849
SeriesJAMES
VideoYouTube ↗

Big idea

Human anger does not produce God's righteousness, so believers must rely on the Holy Spirit to be quick to listen, slow to speak, and slow to anger in order to model Jesus and love others well.

Summary

This sermon examines James 1:19-20, focusing on the call to be quick to listen, slow to speak, and slow to become angry, especially in difficult conversations. Using examples of Jesus rebuking disciples and religious leaders, the preacher shows how those who listened without anger were transformed, while the Pharisees' anger led them to crucify Jesus. Believers are urged to rely on the Holy Spirit rather than their own strength to respond with humility and love when conversations become challenging.

Key points

  • We struggle to listen in difficult conversations, often formulating rebuttals instead
  • Jesus modeled intentional, loving criticism that pointed people toward truth
  • Disciples like Peter and Nicodemus were transformed by receiving hard words without anger
  • The Pharisees' anger toward Jesus led to destructive outcomes
  • Preparation through Scripture, prayer, and community equips us to respond with self-control
