We Are the Church: The Local Church

Length00:46:33

Big idea

The local church is defined not by what we receive but by how we live out the biblical 'one another' commands—loving, forgiving, and bearing with each other in sacrificial unity.

Summary

This sermon examines what it means to be part of a local church, emphasizing that biblical church membership requires actively living out the 'one another' commands of Scripture. Using two contrasting examples—Ahithophel's bitter betrayal of David and the Moravians' radical unity in the 1720s—the preacher illustrates how unforgiveness destroys community while sacrificial commitment to one another produces world-changing revival.

Key points

  • The New Testament word 'church' almost always refers to a local, identifiable body of believers
  • Scripture gives dozens of 'one another' commands that define church life: love, forgive, serve, bear burdens, submit, encourage
  • Ahithophel betrayed David out of unhealed wounds from David's sin against his granddaughter Bathsheba, showing how untransformed wounds spread destruction
  • The Moravians demonstrated radical unity through a 100-year prayer chain, sending 1 in 12 members as missionaries, and influencing the First Great Awakening
  • Jesus said the world will know his disciples by their love for one another
