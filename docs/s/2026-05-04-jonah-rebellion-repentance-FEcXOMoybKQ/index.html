Big idea

God calls us to surrender our self-interest and national pride to participate in His mission of extending mercy and forgiveness to all people, even our enemies.

Summary

This sermon explores Jonah chapter 1, emphasizing that the story is not about a whale or hiding from God, but about a prophet's willful rebellion against God's command to preach repentance to Israel's enemy, Nineveh. Jonah knew God would forgive the Assyrians if they repented, and he refused to participate in that mercy, preferring they be destroyed instead.

Key points

  • Jonah was Jeroboam II's prophet who led Israel in syncretistic idol worship
  • He fled to Tarshish (opposite direction from Nineveh) to frustrate God's plan of mercy
  • The pagan sailors feared God more than the prophet did
  • Jesus used the sign of Jonah to point to resurrection and God's offer of salvation to all nations
  • Israel was destroyed by Assyria because Israel never repented, not because Nineveh did
