Big idea

Christians must reclaim time from addictive screens and create space for stillness, because only in quietness—not constant digital noise—can we hear God's voice and receive the peace He offers.

Summary

This sermon addresses smartphone addiction and announces a 28-day digital detox for the church. It explains how tech companies deliberately design apps to be addictive through dopamine loops, infinite scrolling, and personalized algorithms, and shows the mental health damage this causes—especially anxiety, depression, and reduced brain connectivity. The preacher argues that constant screen time prevents Christians from experiencing the stillness and rest God designed for us.

Key points

  • Smartphones are engineered to addict us through reward loops, infinite scroll, and algorithms
  • Screen addiction correlates with rising rates of depression, anxiety, and suicide
  • Brain scans show smartphone addiction weakens connectivity between emotional regulation and self-control centers
  • God designed us for face-to-face connection and stillness, not constant digital noise
  • The 28-day detox removes distracting apps to reclaim control and hear God's voice
