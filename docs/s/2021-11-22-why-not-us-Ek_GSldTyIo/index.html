Why NOT Us?

Big idea

God specializes in bringing life and renewal to what appears dead and hopeless, and invites us to believe through prayer that He will do it again.

Summary

Using Ezekiel's vision of the valley of dry bones, this sermon argues that God specializes in bringing resurrection and renewal precisely when situations appear most hopeless. Drawing parallels between the Renaissance following the Black Death and potential renewal after COVID-19 and cultural crisis, the preacher calls the church to prayer as 'spiritual activism,' believing God can revive what seems dead.

Key points

  • God's pattern throughout history is resurrection after apparent death - from Egyptian slavery, Babylonian exile, and ultimately Jesus' resurrection
  • God asks 'Can these bones live?' when Israel is in exile and all hope seems lost, inviting them to imagine a different future
  • God often shows up 'late' - after everything has collapsed - because some of His best work happens when things seem completely over
  • Prayer is the starting point for the things only God can do - not inaction but 'spiritual activism' in God's presence
  • Historical examples like the Hebrides revival show what God does when His people pray earnestly, leading to the challenge: 'Why not us?'
