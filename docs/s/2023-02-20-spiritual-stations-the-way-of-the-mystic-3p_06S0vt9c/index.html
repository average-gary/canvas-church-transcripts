Length00:44:49

Big idea

Christian faith is not merely intellectual assent to doctrines but the experiential knowing of God through practices of stillness, surrender, and contemplative engagement with Scripture that strip away our pretense and open us to encounter the divine mystery.

Summary

This sermon explores Christian mysticism as the experiential pursuit of knowing God, not merely understanding doctrines about Him. The speaker argues that encountering the divine mystery requires stripping away pretense and being still before God, as illustrated by Elijah hearing God's 'still small voice' after wind, earthquake, and fire.

Key points

  • Mysticism means desiring to 'touch the divine' and know the mystery of God experientially
  • Christ spoke of mystery in the parable of the sower, granting disciples to 'know' Him, not just understand teachings
  • Elijah encountered God not in dramatic displays but in the 'still small voice' after being stripped of everything
  • Faith is the assurance that Someone is there, not just intellectual agreement with propositions
  • The practice of Lectio Divina (divine reading) provides a way to experience God through slow, contemplative Scripture meditation
