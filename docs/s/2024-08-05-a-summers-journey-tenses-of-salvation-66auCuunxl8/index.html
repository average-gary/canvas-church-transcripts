Big idea

God's salvation is not merely a past transaction but a magnificent, multifaceted work where He brings His enemies into His family through Christ—a reality so profound it deserves lifelong meditation rather than theological reduction.

Summary

This sermon explores Paul's multi-dimensional view of salvation, focusing on the past tense aspect as part of a three-week series. Pastor Ryan argues that American evangelicalism has oversimplified salvation to a single past event, when Scripture presents it as past, present, and future realities that together form God's plan to restore rebellious humanity.

Key points

  • Salvation encompasses three tenses: past (we were saved), present (we are being saved), and future (we will be saved)
  • Past tense salvation itself has three layers: eternity past (God's plan before creation), earthly past (the Fall), and individual past (personal rebellion)
  • Salvation is like adoption: enemies brought into God's family, given legal status, parented into growth, and promised an inheritance
  • We are rebels who want God's possessions without His presence or preferences, yet He runs to us like the father to the prodigal son
  • Believers should not become bored with salvation's beauty but spend a lifetime meditating on its depth and mystery
