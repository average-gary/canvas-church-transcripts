Big idea

Our physical bodies are gifts from God that we are called to steward well as acts of worship, recognizing that what we do with our bodies has spiritual significance while not making physical health or pleasure ultimate.

Summary

This sermon challenges two unbalanced views of the body—hedonism (overemphasizing physical pleasure and health) and Gnosticism (dismissing the body as unimportant)—and calls Christians to 'grateful stewardship,' treating their bodies as gifts from God that matter spiritually. The preacher argues that what we do with our bodies—how we eat, exercise, rest, and use substances—is an act of worship with eternal significance.

Key points

  • Two bad ideas: hedonism elevates physical pleasure/appearance above all; Gnosticism dismisses the body as irrelevant to spiritual life
  • The right view is 'grateful stewardship'—our bodies are gifts from God bought with Christ's blood, to be used for His purposes
  • Taking care of our bodies is spiritual worship, not separate from our faith
  • Practical stewardship includes eating healthy, exercising regularly, resting intentionally, and staying sober-minded
  • Our bodies matter now, but we also look forward to resurrection bodies—balanced hope
