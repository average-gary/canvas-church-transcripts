Big idea

Because Jesus rose from the dead and will one day resurrect believers to inherit a fully restored creation, our present sufferings—no matter how severe—are not worth comparing to the eternal glory that awaits us.

Summary

This sermon explores Paul's teaching on future salvation, arguing that present suffering cannot compare to the coming glory when Christ returns to resurrect believers and restore all creation. The pastor reframes heaven not as a disembodied escape but as bodily resurrection to inherit a redeemed earth—the new heavens and new earth—where creation will finally exist as God intended.

Key points

  • Paul emphasizes salvation in the future tense more than past or present
  • Glory means all creation under God's rule; suffering means creation under human rule, culminating in death
  • All creation groans and waits for the resurrection of God's children
  • Jesus's resurrection proves victory over death and guarantees the redemption of all creation
  • Christians should lament the brokenness of this world while eagerly anticipating bodily resurrection and the new earth
