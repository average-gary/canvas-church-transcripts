Big idea

Christian service builds up the family of God through fellowship that actively participates in believers' needs and hospitality that welcomes strangers as family, all made possible by Christ's sacrificial work at the communion table.

Summary

This sermon concludes an eight-week series on Christian community and service by examining how three tables—fellowship, hospitality, and communion—can transform strangers into neighbors and neighbors into family. The preacher argues that the early church turned the Roman world upside down not through swords but through meals that cultivated kingdom values and sent believers out on mission.

Key points

  • The fellowship table calls Christians to actively participate in each other's needs, not just casual socializing
  • The hospitality table means treating strangers like family with no strings attached, building relational not transactional service
  • The communion table makes both fellowship and hospitality possible through Christ's broken body and shed blood
  • Early Christian tables brought together diverse people across ethnicity, gender, and economic status as equals
  • The goal is moving people from the hospitality table to the fellowship table through relationship and gospel proclamation
