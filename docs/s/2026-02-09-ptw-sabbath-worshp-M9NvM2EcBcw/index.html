Big idea

Sabbath is a day set apart for God where we grow in surrender to Him through experiencing Him as the source of every good gift.

Summary

This sermon concludes a four-week series on Sabbath by arguing that the goal of Sabbath practice is worship—defined as growing in surrender to God. While stopping, resting, and delighting are essential practices, they must lead us to recognize God as the giver of all good gifts, not become ends in themselves.

Key points

  • Sabbath is made 'holy'—set apart for the Lord, not just for moral goodness
  • Worship means surrender: receiving God's gifts and giving them back with open hands
  • We can practice Sabbath wrongly by enjoying the gifts without growing in surrender to the giver
  • God's beauty and pleasure are both gift and map, leading us back to Him
  • Sabbath forms a counter-community with different loyalties in a work-obsessed culture
