Big idea

The church must be a family where forgiveness and reconciliation overcome social divisions, with all members standing equally before God regardless of their earthly status or past wrongs.

Summary

This sermon examines Paul's letter to Philemon about the runaway slave Onesimus, who became a Christian while with Paul. The preacher explores how Paul navigates conflicting legal and theological obligations to advocate for reconciliation, arguing that the church must be a family where all stand equal before God and forgiveness overcomes social divisions.

Key points

  • Paul writes to Philemon asking him to receive his runaway slave Onesimus back as a brother in Christ, not as property
  • The concept of koinonia means the early church held all things in common and stood on equal ground before God regardless of social status
  • Paul carefully appeals to Philemon's relationship with him and commitment to Christ rather than commanding obedience
  • Forgiveness and reconciliation are essential for maintaining Christian fellowship, especially when someone repents
  • Historical tradition suggests Onesimus was freed and later became bishop of Ephesus
