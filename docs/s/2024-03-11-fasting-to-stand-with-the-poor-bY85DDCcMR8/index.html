Big idea

The fast God desires is not religious ritual for personal gain but self-denial that frees us to break oppressive chains and genuinely serve the poor and vulnerable.

Summary

This sermon examines fasting as a spiritual discipline for standing with the poor, moving beyond virtue signaling to genuine self-denial and service. Using Isaiah 58, Sam Rogers argues that God rejects selfish fasting done for personal blessing and instead calls believers to fast from self-love by breaking chains of oppression they themselves create and actively caring for the hungry, homeless, and vulnerable. The sermon emphasizes that true fasting strengthens believers to fight injustice, relate to those who suffer, and give practically from what they deny themselves.

Key points

  • God rejects selfish fasting done to manipulate blessing
  • True fasting means breaking chains of oppression we create, not just what others do
  • Fasting helps us relate to hunger, give what we save, and fight injustice
  • Jesus exemplified perfect self-emptying humility
  • Serve the vulnerable people God has placed within arm's reach
