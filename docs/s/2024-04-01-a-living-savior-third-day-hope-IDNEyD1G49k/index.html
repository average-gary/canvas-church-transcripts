Big idea

Jesus doesn't rescue us from the pain of day one disappointment or day two confusion, but through His resurrection He meets us in the shadows with day three hope that transforms our graves into gardens and gives meaning to our suffering.

Summary

This Easter sermon explores how the resurrection offers hope in the midst of disappointment and despair by walking through the emotional journey of Jesus's early followers across three pivotal days: Good Friday's crushing disappointment of unmet expectations, Silent Saturday's confusion and coping, and Resurrection Sunday's transformative hope. The preacher connects the disciples' experiences to contemporary struggles with pain and loss, then examines how different followers—Judas, Thomas, Peter, and Saul—responded to encounters with the risen Christ.

Key points

  • Good Friday brought the disciples devastating disappointment when their expectations of Jesus as political Messiah were shattered
  • Silent Saturday represents the confusion and unhealthy coping mechanisms we use when faced with life's pain
  • Resurrection Sunday offers 'day three hope'—not elimination of suffering, but hope and meaning within it
  • Jesus turns graves into gardens, redeeming our pain rather than simply removing it
  • Different people need different encounters with the risen Christ: the despairing need to hear they're loved, doubters need to keep showing up, the wayward need to come home, and the religious need a personal relationship beyond rules
