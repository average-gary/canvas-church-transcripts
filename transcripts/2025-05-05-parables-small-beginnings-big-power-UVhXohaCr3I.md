# PARABLES: Small Beginnings, Big Power

*2025-05-05 — [watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UVhXohaCr3I) — 01:13:49*

> **The kingdom of heaven begins as something small and hidden that the world overlooks, but once planted it grows with wild, disruptive, unstoppable power that transforms everything from the inside out.**

## Summary

This sermon explores two parables from Matthew 13 that describe the kingdom of heaven as small, hidden beginnings that grow into unstoppable, transformative power. Using the images of a mustard seed (an invasive plant no farmer would plant) and leaven (yeast hidden in flour), Jesus reveals that God's kingdom doesn't arrive through worldly power or respectability but spreads quietly, disruptively, and inevitably through faithful obedience.

**Key points**

- The mustard seed represents the wild, invasive, uncontrollable nature of God's kingdom—small but unstoppable once planted
- The kingdom is not safe or domesticated; it disrupts expectations and spreads beyond human control
- Leaven illustrates how the kingdom works hidden but thoroughly, transforming everything it touches from the inside out
- Believers must test all 'leaven' against Jesus' teachings, as false teaching can also spread quietly
- Faithfulness in small, hidden acts of obedience matters—God works beneath the surface in ways we cannot see

**Scripture:** Matthew 13:31-35, Ezekiel 17:22-24, Daniel 4, 1 Corinthians 15, 1 Corinthians 5, 1 Corinthians 3:6, Isaiah 55:11, Matthew 24:24, Galatians 6:9
